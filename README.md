# Khaurvaezynth

> The ivory rook guards a square that belongs to neither night nor morning.

## A Game Beside the Window

The chessboard stands beside a window overlooking an empty courtyard. Its squares are pale stone and dark glass. The pieces are ordinary except for one ivory rook, taller than the others, with a narrow door carved into its base.

No one remembers beginning the game. The board was discovered in a house after every room had been emptied. Two chairs remained beside it. One was warm, and the other carried a little dust shaped like the outline of a folded coat.

The caretaker visits each morning. She opens the window, brushes the dust from the pieces, and records the position of the rook in a book without dates. Its square changes whenever somebody in the courtyard remembers a dream.

She has never caught it moving. Sometimes, however, she hears a small door close while her back is turned.

## The Player With a Missing Piece

A man arrived carrying an ivory pawn wrapped in a handkerchief. He said it had appeared on his table during a game he was playing alone. Afterward, every other piece on his board had cast a shadow toward it.

The caretaker placed his pawn beside the rook. The carved door opened slightly. Through it could be seen a long hall lined with windows, and through each window a different version of the empty courtyard.

The man recognized one of them. It contained a tree that had stood outside his father's house, its branches heavy with fruit no member of the family had ever tasted. Beneath the tree sat his father, studying a chessboard balanced across his knees.

The pawn grew warm. The man reached toward the door, but the caretaker touched his wrist and asked him which side he had been playing.

## The Square Under the Square

He could not answer. He remembered moving white pieces and black pieces, turning the board between moves so that each army approached him in turn. He remembered an empty chair opposite his own and the habit of waiting before making a reply.

The caretaker lifted the rook. Beneath it was a square of clear glass, although the board had contained no such square moments before. Through the glass, a great eye watched the room from below.

Its pupil held a courtyard, and in that courtyard a tree grew from the middle of a chessboard. The creature around the eye could not be seen completely. A curve of horn passed beneath the neighboring squares. Farther away, scales caught the window's light like rooftops after rain.

The man set his pawn against the glass. The eye closed, and a new piece appeared in the empty chair's reflection.

## The Game Inside the Tower

The rook's door widened. The hall beyond it led to a round room whose walls were covered with narrow shelves. On every shelf stood a chessboard. Each board carried a single piece, and beneath each piece lay a small folded note.

The caretaker knew the room from her book. She had drawn it many times while asleep, always leaving one shelf empty. Now the ivory pawn rose from the glass square and passed through the door without being touched.

It came to rest on the empty shelf. Its folded note opened. The man saw his father's handwriting and remembered the last unfinished game they had left beside the tree.

Under the house, the scaled creature shifted. The courtyard outside filled briefly with the shadows of branches, though no tree stood there.

## The Move Nobody Made

Around the rook's upper rim, letters appeared between its ivory battlements:

*Khaur — vae — zynth — oruun.*

The caretaker wrote them in her book. The man's father looked up from the board within the carved doorway. He did not seem surprised to see them, but moved one chair farther from the tree, leaving space for another.

The man searched the visible board for a move he could make. Every square was occupied by light, shadow, or something he had not yet learned to recognize. At last he took his empty handkerchief and laid it across the table.

Inside the rook, his father folded the note and placed it beneath the pawn. The new chair became warm. Beneath the glass square, the creature's eye opened onto a sky bright with unfamiliar stars.

## The Position After Silence

The carved door closed. The rook returned to the board, now no taller than the other pieces. The man's handkerchief contained a small fruit from the tree he had never been allowed to climb.

He thanked the caretaker and left before the courtyard shadows faded. At home, his solitary chessboard stood exactly where he had left it. The empty chair opposite him had been turned slightly toward the window.

The caretaker continues to record the rook's position. Her book now contains dates, but they belong to no calendar she recognizes. She leaves the window open longer each morning.

Somewhere beneath the squares, an enormous wing rests across a house filled with unfinished games.

And beside the ivory rook, a tiny folded note waits for a hand that no longer needs to choose a side.
